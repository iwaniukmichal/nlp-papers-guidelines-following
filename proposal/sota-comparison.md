## 2. Individual Study Descriptions (What / Data / Result)

1. **Gundersen & Kjensmo (2018):** Manually audited $n = 400$ papers ($325$ empirical, $75$ non-empirical; $100$ per conference edition) from AAAI (2014, 2016) and IJCAI (2013, 2016) across fixed documentation variables grouped into Experiment, Data, and Method factors. None ($0.0\%$) documented all variables required for full reproduction, with only $20\%\text{–}30\%$ of variables reported on average, establishing the foundational design precedent for longitudinal paper-level reporting rubrics.
2. **Wieling et al. (2018):** Audited code and data links across $n = 395$ full papers ($164$ at ACL 2011 and $231$ at ACL 2016), followed by author email outreach and 8-hour reproduction attempts on a 10-paper subsample. While code sharing increased from $33.1\%$ (2011) to $59.3\%$ (2016) and data sharing from $75.8\%$ to $86.3\%$, only $50.0\%$ (5/10) were approximately reproduced and $10.0\%$ (1/10) exactly reproduced.
3. **Dror et al. (2018):** Audited statistical significance testing across $n = 213$ empirical papers ($180$ at ACL 2017 and $33$ at TACL 2017, out of $233$ total papers). Only $35.0\%$ of ACL papers and $54.55\%$ of TACL papers reported significance tests, $85.71\%$ of ACL tests used a valid named procedure, and just $2.73\%$ (3/110) of multi-dataset ACL papers applied multiple-testing corrections (vs. $21.05\%$ in TACL).
4. **Dodge et al. (2019):** Manually audited a random sample of $n = 50$ experimental modeling papers from EMNLP 2018 against an experimental reporting checklist. While $92\%$ reported train/dev/test splits and $74\%$ reported best hyperparameters, only $30\%$ shared code, $24\%$ reported validation scores, $18\%$ specified compute infrastructure, and $8\%$ reported hyperparameter search bounds.
5. **Howcroft et al. (2020):** Used PRISMA selection and solo/duo consensus coding on $n = 165$ human evaluation papers ($478$ quality criterion assessments screened from $578$ papers) at INLG and ENLG (2000–2019). They found $58.37\%$ omitted criterion definitions, $65.06\%$ omitted evaluator prompts, and Krippendorff's $\alpha$ ranged from $0.08$ to $0.73$ (solo $\alpha = 0.12$ on normalized criteria; duo consensus $\alpha \le 0.64$).
6. **Belz et al. (2021):** Conducted a systematic review of $n = 62$ reproducibility and replication studies in NLP across the ACL Anthology and adjacent ML venues up to 2020, synthesizing $513$ comparable reproduction outcome pairs. Only $14.03\%$ ($77/513$ cases) yielded an exact/identical score match to the original paper, while documenting a sharp post-2017 rise peaking in 2020 around REPROLANG and persistent compute/hyperparameter barriers.
7. **Rogers et al. (2021):** Analyzed legal and ethical tensions in NLP data collection and reuse (copyright, GDPR privacy, informed consent, and licensing) across *ACL practices to design a standardized peer-review checklist for responsible data use. This work directly established the data and asset licensing items of the ARR Responsible NLP Checklist (including our rubric item B2 on asset licensing and C1 on compute).
8. **Shmueli et al. (2021):** Scanned $6,776$ accepted papers across 16 ACL, EMNLP, and NAACL proceedings (2015–2020) and manually coded $n = 703$ papers ($10.4\%$) employing human crowdworkers. Only $17.4\%$ (122/703) reported worker compensation details and $2.0\%$ (14/703) mentioned Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval or exemption.
9. **Marie et al. (2021):** Manually audited $n = 769$ machine translation (MT) evaluation papers across six *ACL venues (ACL, NAACL, EACL, EMNLP, CoNLL, AACL 2010–2020) using a 6-criterion credibility rubric. While $98.8\%$ reported BLEU ($74.3\%$ BLEU-only overall; $82.1\%$ in 2019–2020), $<10\%$ included human evaluation, $\le 65.0\%$ used significance testing, and $\sim 40\%$ (2019–2020) copied baseline scores without verifying identical data splits.
10. **Benotti & Blackburn (2022):** Collaboratively classified all $n = 90$ Ethics Consideration Sections (ECS) found among $572$ ACL 2021 long papers ($15.7\%$ section prevalence). Within those 90 sections, $74.4\%$ (67/90) mentioned research benefits, $52.2\%$ (47/90) discussed potential harms, and $23.3\%$ (21/90) addressed vulnerable groups.
11. **Arvan et al. (2022):** Scraped longitudinal code-sharing rates across all papers in ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, LREC, and COLING (2016–2022) and attempted full reproduction of $n = 8$ randomly sampled EMNLP 2021 papers. Although code sharing exceeded $50\%\text{–}70\%$ in checklist-adopting venues ($723/1,300+$ papers, $\sim 55\%$, at EMNLP 2021), only $25.0\%$ (2/8) of sampled EMNLP 2021 papers could be fully reproduced out of the box.
12. **Ducel et al. (2022):** Audited compliance with the #BenderRule (naming the studied language) across $n \approx 14,000$ LREC (2000–2020) and ACL (1979–2020) articles, combining $\sim 1,000$ manual gold annotations with rule-based and ML classifiers. With human Cohen's $\kappa = 0.979$ and classifier $\text{F1} = 0.976$, compliance reached $80\%\text{–}90\%$ at LREC versus $60\%\text{–}70\%$ at ACL ($30\%\text{–}40\%$ non-compliant).
13. **Hershcovich et al. (2022):** Combined regex extraction across *ACL (2016–2022) with a manual audit of $n = 100$ randomly sampled EMNLP 2021 deep learning papers across five climate-reporting dimensions. In the EMNLP 2021 sample ($n=100$), $28\%$ reported training duration, $13\%$ released public weights, $3\%$ disclosed compute location, and $0\%$ reported energy use or greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions.
14. **Magnusson et al. (2023):** Analyzed $n = 10,405$ anonymous author responses to the ACL Reproducibility Checklist (2020–2021) alongside review scores. They found increased post-checklist reporting of compute, validation scores, and hyperparameters; $46\%$ claimed open-source code ($+8\%$ reviewer reproducibility score), while $44\%$ collected new data ($-5\%$ acceptance rate, $-2\%$ reproducibility score).
15. **Rohatgi et al. (2023):** Constructed the ACL Open Computational Linguistics (ACL OCL) corpus covering $n = 73,285$ structured full-text papers and $210,000$ extracted figures from the ACL Anthology up to September 2022 using GROBID, providing a structured baseline to cross-check PDF section parsing before EMNLP 2022.
16. **Xiao et al. (2023):** Evaluated codebook-guided deductive qualitative coding with GPT-3 against human expert benchmarks on $n = 668$ children's curiosity-driven questions across two tasks. Supplying structured code definitions with examples achieved Cohen's $\kappa = 0.61$ (substantial agreement) on binary question complexity and $\kappa = 0.38$ (fair agreement) on 4-class syntactic structure.
17. **Yang et al. (2024):** Audited $n = 7,433$ non-empty Hugging Face dataset cards (from $24,065$ repositories up to March 2023) using keyword matching, BERT regression, and 5 PhD annotators. Full 5-section compliance was $29.0\%$ overall ($86.0\%$ for top-100 downloaded vs. $7.9\%$ for 0-download datasets), with "Considerations for Using the Data" filled in just $8.0\%$ of cards.
18. **Karamolegkou et al. (2025):** Introduced the EthiCon corpus of $n = 1,580$ ethics statements extracted from $8,048$ ACL 2022–2023 papers ($480$ in ACL 2022, $1,100$ in ACL 2023), annotated across five categories and benchmarked with four open-weight LLMs. Human annotators reached Cohen's $\kappa = 0.77$ and LLMs reached $0.81\text{–}0.83$ BERTScore F1; in ACL 2023 ($n=1,100$), $67.18\%$ of statements were boilerplate disclaimers, $40.36\%$ listed specific concerns, $36.64\%$ described mitigation actions, and $>33\%$ claimed no ethical concerns existed.
19. **Thaepprasit et al. (2026):** Extracted and analyzed $n = 16,067$ self-reported *Limitations* sections across ACL and EMNLP (2020–2025) using a human-AI hybrid coding framework (Gemini) and manually audited $n = 136$ post-mandate papers lacking an extracted section. Dedicated section prevalence rose from $<1\%$ pre-mandate (2020–2021) to $>99\%$ post-mandate (EMNLP 2022+); among the $136$ flagged post-mandate papers, $41$ ($0.26\%$ of the post-mandate corpus) were parser extraction failures and $95$ ($0.60\%$) truly lacked a standalone section ($50$ embedded limitations inside other sections, $45$ omitted them entirely). Their human-AI coder achieved $\text{F1} = 0.801$ and Krippendorff's $\alpha = 0.647$ (vs. human baseline $\alpha = 0.644$).
20. **Jinnath & Zhao (2026):** Audited $n = 73,922$ public Responsible NLP Checklist responses and justifications from EMNLP 2025 Main and Findings tracks. They found that $44.9\%$ of `No` justifications were poor or bad-faith (empty or placeholder text), $6.0\%$ of checklists contained parent–child logical contradictions, and $53.0\%$ dismissed potential social or ethical risks outright.
21. **Mohapatra & Pruthi (2026):** Audited $n = 13,867$ AI-usage disclosure statements from EMNLP 2025 and ICLR 2026, validating a Gemini 2.5 Flash evaluator against three human annotators on $n = 100$ gold statements. While $40.0\%$ of EMNLP 2025 papers and $64.0\%$ of ICLR 2026 papers disclosed AI use, $98.0\%$ (EMNLP) and $77.0\%$ (ICLR) omitted required declarations of human accountability; Gemini 2.5 Flash achieved micro-$\text{F1} = 0.965$ (details) and $0.906$ (tasks), exceeding pairwise human micro-$\text{F1}$ ($0.943$ and $0.888$).
22. **Kunilovskaya et al. (2026):** Audited human annotation reporting across $n = 1,603$ *ACL papers ($2,667$ annotation tasks, 2018–2025) using regex section chunking and Gemini-3.1-Pro validated against 12 expert annotators on $n = 41$ gold papers ($72$ tasks) across 26 categories. Gemini-3.1-Pro achieved Krippendorff's $\alpha = 0.606$ (raw agreement $68.8\%\text{–}96.9\%$), slightly outperforming human–human reliability ($\alpha = 0.585$; raw agreement $64.8\%\text{–}98.6\%$).
23. **Pattnayak & Bhatia (2026):** Conducted a dual-annotator 8-artifact rubric audit of $n = 55$ multi-stage LLM pipeline papers (*ACL, ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML, arXiv 2022–2025) and attempted pipeline reconstructions on a $n = 20$ subsample. Achieving macro Cohen's $\kappa = 0.78$, they found $75\%$ omitted randomness controls, $61\%$ omitted execution traces, and $40\%$ omitted prompt templates, yielding a mean Reproducibility Risk Index (RRI) of $3.2/8$ ($35\%$ high risk).

\section{Significance and State of the Art}
\label{sec:sota}

Table~\ref{tab:sota} summarises 23 prior audits of reporting, reproducibility, and documentation across natural language processing (NLP) and artificial intelligence (AI), detailing each study's corpus, publication split, sample size ($n$), methodology, and quantitative outcomes.

\paragraph{Experimental Reproducibility and Code Audits.}
\newcite{gundersen2018state} manually audited $n=400$ AAAI (2014, 2016) and IJCAI (2013, 2016) papers ($325$ empirical, $75$ non-empirical), finding $0.0\%$ full compliance and only $20\%\text{--}30\%$ mean documentation coverage across Experiment, Data, and Method variables. In NLP, \newcite{wieling2018reproducibility} audited $n=395$ ACL papers ($164$ in 2011, $231$ in 2016), showing code sharing rose from $33.1\%$ to $59.3\%$ and data sharing from $75.8\%$ to $86.3\%$, yet 8-hour reproduction succeeded exactly for only $10.0\%$ ($1/10$) of tested papers. \newcite{belz2021systematic} systematically reviewed $n=62$ NLP reproducibility studies ($513$ comparable score pairs up to 2020), finding that only $14.03\%$ ($77/513$) reproduced identical scores. Similarly, \newcite{arvan2022reproducibility} scraped code links across five conferences (2016--2022)---finding code sharing exceeded $50\%\text{--}70\%$ in checklist venues ($723/1,300+$ papers, $\sim 55\%$, at EMNLP 2021)---but achieved full reproduction for only $25.0\%$ ($2/8$) of sampled EMNLP 2021 papers. For multi-stage large language model (LLM) pipelines (2022--2025), \newcite{pattnayak2026reporting} dual-coded $n=55$ papers (human macro Cohen's $\kappa=0.78$) and reconstructed $20$, finding $75\%$ omitted randomness controls and $61\%$ omitted execution traces (mean Reproducibility Risk Index $3.2/8$).

\paragraph{Evaluation Rigor, Checklists, and Compute Reporting.}
\newcite{dror2018hitchhiker} audited $n=213$ empirical papers at ACL 2017 ($180$) and TACL 2017 ($33$), finding significance testing in only $35.0\%$ of ACL and $54.55\%$ of TACL papers, and multiple-testing corrections in $2.73\%$ ($3/110$) of ACL papers. In machine translation (MT), \newcite{marie2021scientific} audited $n=769$ papers across six *ACL venues (2010--2020), finding $98.8\%$ reported BLEU ($74.3\%$ exclusively; $82.1\%$ in 2019--2020), $<10\%$ included human evaluation, and $\sim 40\%$ (2019--2020) copied baseline scores without verifying identical splits. \newcite{dodge2019show} audited $n=50$ EMNLP 2018 modeling papers, showing high reporting for data splits ($92\%$) and best hyperparameters ($74\%$), but low reporting for code ($30\%$), validation scores ($24\%$), compute infrastructure ($18\%$), and hyperparameter search bounds ($8\%$). Examining environmental and compute reporting in $n=100$ EMNLP 2021 deep learning papers, \newcite{hershcovich2022towards} found $28\%$ reported training duration, $13\%$ released model weights, $3\%$ reported compute location, and $0\%$ reported energy or greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. Following the 2020 introduction of the ACL Reproducibility Checklist, \newcite{magnusson2023reproducibility} analysed $n=10,405$ anonymous submission responses, documenting increased post-checklist compute and hyperparameter reporting, $46\%$ code-sharing claims ($+8\%$ reviewer reproducibility score), and $44\%$ new-data collection ($-5\%$ acceptance rate). Their distinction between mandatory answers and optional practices directly informs our rubric.

\paragraph{Data Documentation, Human Evaluation, and Language Naming.}
\newcite{rogers2021just} synthesised legal and ethical requirements for NLP data reuse at EMNLP 2021, establishing the data and asset-licensing items of the ACL Rolling Review (ARR) Responsible NLP Checklist (the origin of our item B2 on asset licensing). Auditing $n=7,433$ Hugging Face dataset cards (from $24,065$ repositories up to March 2023), \newcite{yang2024navigating} found $29.0\%$ full 5-section compliance ($86.0\%$ for top-100 downloaded vs.\ $7.9\%$ for 0-download datasets) and only $8.0\%$ completion of data-use considerations. For human evaluation and annotation, \newcite{howcroft2020twenty} audited $n=165$ natural language generation (NLG) papers ($478$ criteria, 2000--2019), finding $58.37\%$ omitted criterion definitions and $65.06\%$ omitted prompts (Krippendorff's $\alpha=0.08\text{--}0.73$). \newcite{shmueli2021beyond} scanned $6,776$ ACL/EMNLP/NAACL papers (2015--2020) and coded $n=703$ crowdsourcing studies, where only $17.4\%$ ($122/703$) reported worker pay and $2.0\%$ ($14/703$) reported Institutional Review Board (IRB) oversight. At corpus scale, \newcite{kunilovskaya2026who} audited $n=1,603$ *ACL papers ($2,667$ tasks, 2018--2025) using regex chunking and Gemini-3.1-Pro validated on $n=41$ gold papers ($72$ tasks, 12 experts), where human--LLM agreement ($\alpha=0.606$) slightly exceeded human--human reliability ($\alpha=0.585$). For language naming (\#BenderRule), \newcite{ducel2022do} annotated $\sim 1,000$ articles and classified $n\approx 14,000$ LREC (2000--2020) and ACL (1979--2020) papers ($\kappa=0.979$, classifier $\text{F1}=0.976$), finding $80\%\text{--}90\%$ compliance at LREC vs.\ $60\%\text{--}70\%$ at ACL. To support structured full-text extraction up to September 2022, \newcite{rohatgi2023acl} released the ACL Open Computational Linguistics (ACL OCL) corpus of $n=73,285$ GROBID-parsed papers, providing a reference to cross-check our Docling parsing on 2020--2022 papers.

\paragraph{Ethics, Limitations, Checklist Consistency, and AI Disclosures.}
\newcite{benotti2022ethics} classified all $n=90$ Ethics Consideration Sections (ECS) among $572$ ACL 2021 long papers ($15.7\%$ prevalence), finding $74.4\%$ ($67/90$) discussed benefits, $52.2\%$ ($47/90$) harms, and $23.3\%$ ($21/90$) vulnerable groups. Extending this, \newcite{karamolegkou2025ethical} introduced EthiCon, a human-annotated corpus of $n=1,580$ ethics statements from $8,048$ ACL 2022--2023 papers ($\kappa=0.77$; open-weight LLM BERTScore $\text{F1}=0.81\text{--}0.83$), showing that $67.18\%$ of ACL 2023 statements ($n=1,100$) were boilerplate disclaimers and $>33\%$ claimed no ethical risks. Auditing $n=73,922$ public Responsible NLP Checklist responses and justifications at EMNLP 2025, \newcite{jinnath2026checklist} found $44.9\%$ of ``No'' justifications were poor or bad-faith, $6.0\%$ of checklists contained parent--child contradictions, and $53.0\%$ dismissed societal risks. For AI-writing disclosures, \newcite{mohapatra2026expectations} audited $n=13,867$ statements at EMNLP 2025 ($40.0\%$ disclosure rate) and ICLR 2026 ($64.0\%$) with Gemini 2.5 Flash validated on $n=100$ triple-annotated statements (micro-$\text{F1}=0.965$ on details vs.\ human $0.943$), finding $98.0\%$ of EMNLP disclosures omitted human-accountability declarations.

Most directly comparable to our longitudinal design, \newcite{thaeprasit2026limits} extracted $n=16,067$ self-reported \emph{Limitations} sections from ACL and EMNLP (2020--2025) and combined human coding with a Gemini evaluator ($\text{F1}=0.801$, Krippendorff's $\alpha=0.647$ vs.\ human $\alpha=0.644$), building on codebook-guided deductive LLM coding \cite{xiao2023supporting}, which achieved $\kappa=0.61$ (complexity) and $\kappa=0.38$ (syntax) on $n=668$ questions. \newcite{thaeprasit2026limits} show that dedicated \emph{Limitations} section prevalence rose from $<1\%$ pre-mandate (2020--2021) to $>99\%$ post-mandate (EMNLP 2022+); their manual audit of $n=136$ post-mandate papers lacking an extracted section separated parser extraction failures ($41$ papers, $0.26\%$ of the post-mandate corpus) from truly missing standalone sections ($95$ papers, $0.60\%$: $50$ embedded limitations elsewhere and $45$ omitted them entirely). We adopt this distinction between structural section presence, substantive content, and extraction failure, and extend it to a multi-family longitudinal audit across EMNLP 2020--2026 against versioned historical obligations.
