From guidelines to practice: do NLP papers follow reporting requirements?

An auditable compliance study of EMNLP 2020–2026 Main and Findings papers.
Mid-term presentation: state of the art and proof of concept.

Michał Iwaniuk  ·  Bartłomiej Borycki  ·  Miłosz Chodań  ·  Mikołaj Guzik
Supervisor: Filip Kucia  ·  NLP course 2026/27, Warsaw University of Technology  ·  Topic 4

Problem & research questions

Motivation

Venues keep adding reporting rules. Do the published papers actually follow them?

  • 2020NLP Reproducibility Checklist at submissionSelf-reported answers, not published with the paper
  • 2021Ethics review and ethics/broader-impact discussionEthics committees at NAACL, ACL and EMNLP
  • 2022EMNLP makes a dedicated Limitations section mandatoryLater adopted across ACL Rolling Review
  • 2023+ACL policy on AI writing assistance; Responsible NLP checklistEMNLP 2025 publishes the checklists with the papers

RQ1How does compliance vary by year and by reporting item?

RQ2How does adoption change after a requirement is introduced or strengthened?

H1: adoption of a fixed indicator increases after an explicit mandate.

RQ3How does reporting in 2023–2026 differ from 2020–2022?

Exploratory and descriptive. Policy and era effects overlap, so no causal claims.

Unit of analysis: one paper × reporting item. Compliance means an applicable obligation is satisfied by evidence in the paper or its ordinary appendices.

From state of the art to our design

Gap

What the literature tells us, and what we do with it

Presence ≠ contentDisclaimers: 20% of ethics sections (Benotti), 67% of ethics statements (EthiCon), 44.9% of "No" justifications (Jinnath)
→
Separate structural items (heading) from substantive items (specific content)
Self-reports overestimateChecklist answers are not checked against the paper (Magnusson)
→
We audit the paper text and quote evidence; checklist pages are excluded
Mandates change structure fastLimitations sections: <10% → ~98–100% (Thaepprasit)
→
Test H1 on fixed indicators before and after each dated mandate
LLM coding works, if validatedCodebook + examples: F1 0.80, α on par with humans (Xiao, Thaepprasit)
→
One frozen LLM, checked against double human annotation on a held-out sample
Parsing fails silently41 of 136 "missing" sections were parser errors (Thaepprasit)
→
Parse failures and unclear applicability become Uncertain, never Non-compliant
Obligations change every yearChecklists, ethics policy and AI policy all appear at different times
→
A dated policy register: compliance is judged against the rules of that year
Gap: prior work audits one item (limitations, ethics, code) or self-reports. We audit several items in the paper text against the obligations valid in each year, with applicability and human validation.

Proof of concept · setup

PoC

Pilot: 14 papers, two evaluators, the same rubric

14 × 6
Pilot sample = 84 paper–item pairs
One Main and one Findings paper per year, 2020–2026, drawn at random from the eligible manifest.
  • Seed 42, inclusion probabilities stored per paper
  • Frozen sample file; PDFs re-downloadable from the Anthology
  • Same papers are now being double-annotated by humans
Rules
Baseline: rule_baseline_v1
Deterministic regular expressions over the parsed text, no cost.
  • Headings: "Limitations", "Ethics Statement", "Broader Impact"
  • Compute: GPU-hours, or hardware names only (= inadequate)
  • Licence names (CC-BY, MIT, Apache 2.0…), AI-tool mentions
LLM
Run 1: gemini-2.5-flash
Full paper text (≤ 120k characters, with page markers) and the whole rubric in one prompt.
  • Temperature 0, JSON output for all 6 items
  • Per item: applicability, evidence status, verbatim quote, location, justification
  • Every quote is checked against the extracted text

Each run is archived in runs/2026-11-15_pilot_14_* with its config (model, temperature, prompt SHA-256, rubric 1.0.0-poc). Both evaluators derive compliance with the same shared function, using the mandate years recorded in the rubric.

Proof of concept · what we learned

Insights & limitations

The pipeline works end to end; the open question is validity

Observations on the pilot (n = 14, descriptive)

  • The mandate shows even here: a dedicated Limitations section in 0 of 4 papers from 2020–21 and 10 of 10 from 2022–26.
  • Rules miss evidence without a heading. The LLM found a compute budget in a 2021 Findings paper:
    "…a machine with 4xV100 32G GPUs… Total training time…"
  • The LLM uses applicability: a 2021 position paper gets N/A for compute and licensing, where rules say "not reported".
  • Licensing: 9 of 14 papers mention reused assets without naming a licence. AI assistance is disclosed in 1 of 14.

Cost and runtime (Gemini run 1)

  • ~22k prompt tokens and ~16.5 s per paper; whole pilot ≈ $0.08
  • Projected full corpus (16,618 papers): ≈ $95, or ≈ $47 with the batch API

Limitations of the PoC

  • No human reference yet: agreement between evaluators is not accuracy. We cannot yet say which evaluator is right.
  • n = 14: no statistical claims; the pilot checks the pipeline and the workload.
  • Rubric 1.0.0-poc is not frozen. The I1 rule ("embedded in Conclusion = inadequate") still has to be checked against each year's policy wording.
  • I4–I6 have no verified mandate, so every paper gets compliance N/A; only presence is tracked for them.
  • One model, one run, no repeats. The parser version was not stored in these archived runs; new runs record it.
